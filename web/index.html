Abs, obliques & core

Central abs ↔ obliques ↔ lower back, with abdominal links to the pelvis. Choose a region and side, then pulse or grab a front-map dot. Higher support and bracing resist deformation; bracing stiffens the abdomen and lower back together. Obliques combine the lateral muscle layers; deep transversus activity is approximated by bracing. Core flexion is controlled under Muscle-driven movement.

Upper back, shoulders & hands

Upper back → shoulders → arms → hands, with deformation traveling both ways on the same side. Select a region and pulse to see its neighbors respond. Hands stay firmer with a 12 mm tissue movement limit. Support controls are muscle/tendon response proxies; joint motion is controlled under Muscle-driven movement.

Buttock & lower-back response

Choose Buttocks or Lower back to touch one region and watch its neighbor respond. Coupling transfers movement both ways on the same side; 0% removes these links. Lower-back support approximates the stabilizing role of erector spinae and multifidus. Auto follows the side you touch; Both sides is explicit. These are surface response proxies.

About this simulation

Illustrative surface dynamics and muscle-actuator joint motion on adult anatomical meshes. Muscle groups and antagonists drive simplified angular dynamics, not measured anatomical muscle paths or full-body balance. Pelvis and legs stay supported; this is not a ragdoll. Muscle support changes stiffness and shape; muscles and fat are not separate anatomical layers. No tissue self-collision, internal anatomy, or calibrated biological properties. Use this for visual experiments, not medical predictions. Path tracing and Pause physics hold both joints and tissue.

Models and skin: MakeHuman / MPFB. External anatomy: MHX2. Asset credits and license details are included. Credits
